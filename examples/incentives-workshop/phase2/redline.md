### Edit 1 — Abstract: add the design-stage-selection scope qualifier
*Answers F-057, F-066, F-044, F-058 (corroborated by 5 seats). fix-safety: prose-only, safe.*

> Our results suggest that increasing financial rewards rarely produces large performance gains in the
> experimental settings most studied by economists.**​ Because this literature tends, by design, to avoid
> contexts where incentives are expected to have large effects (Section 3), we read this as a statement
> about the contexts economists have chosen to study rather than about incentive effectiveness in
> general.**

*Why:* the general policy reading lives in the abstract while the scope limitation is on p.70; this puts
the qualifier where the takeaway forms. No quantity changes.

### Edit 2 — Heterogeneity: foreground the dependence-aware averaging
*Answers F-086, F-030, F-101, F-102 (corroborated by 5 seats). fix-safety: prose re-emphasis is safe; the
numeric promotion of Table 5 to the frequentist results is **deferred** (needs a re-run).*

> The Bayesian approach, however, treats all observations as independent, and to account for within-study
> dependence we also employ frequentist model averaging with Mallow's weights (Hansen, 2007) and
> orthogonalization of model space according to Amini & Parmeter (2012).**​ Because most studies
> contribute many correlated estimates, we treat the dependence-aware frequentist averaging as the
> primary basis for our heterogeneity conclusions and report the Bayesian inclusion probabilities
> alongside it for comparison.**

*Why:* the headline moderator ranking and implied effects should be anchored on the method that respects
the nesting of 2,193 estimates in 88 studies. (Re-deriving Table 5 from the frequentist results is an
author re-run, not done here.)

### Edit 3 — Publication bias: state MAIVE's exclusion restriction as an assumption
*Answers F-053, F-121. fix-safety: prose-only, safe.*

> Such an instrument is correlated with the standard error by the definition of the latter and can be
> expected to be less related to method choices.**​ We treat the instrument's independence from method
> choices as an identifying assumption rather than a tested property; absent a formal falsification of
> this exclusion restriction in our sample, we read MAIVE as one member of the correction battery rather
> than a decisive arbiter of the corrected effect.**

*Why:* tempers the "preferred" framing to match what is actually demonstrated, without removing MAIVE.
