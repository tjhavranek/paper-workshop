## The chair’s verdict

> Brutal register, delivery only — the must-fix set below is identical to what a supportive register would return. This artifact is a well-architected design document whose central claim is not established by anything inside it. Every headline guarantee — "trust a 300-agent run," "better than any conference," "fabrication structurally impossible," "every argument, every word a checked invariant," "impeccable revised paper" — is asserted in the present indicative while the only procedure that could measure recall or false-positive rate is, by the document's own words, "parallel, non-blocking ... not as a gate" and has never been run. Worse, several of these absolutes are refuted by the document's own constitution: rule 10 ("an LLM verifier shares the hallucination it is checking") defeats the multi-angle panel for the six non-code angles and contradicts "no fabricated results — ever"; rule 15 (a run may be degraded) contradicts "impeccable"; and the one deterministic gate the whole same-model-trust story leans on (quote_gate.py) fails OPEN on an empty-quote non-absence finding, verified in code. The validity verdict caps the headline at the floor: multiple un-rebutted High findings of the correctness/integrity and fatal-overclaim kind stand, and the flagship demonstration is the tool auditing its own design with no mandatory independent eye. The design is genuinely promising; the evidence and the calibrated claims to match it do not yet exist.

**Validity verdict.** The central claim — that the rails make a 300-600-agent same-model run trustworthy enough to act on, 'better than any conference,' without laundering a fatal flaw into a confident green light — is NOT supported by anything in the manuscript, and is partially refuted by the manuscript's own constitution. Three independent, un-rebutted lines establish this and each alone caps the headline at the floor. (1) Evidence: every effectiveness and comparative-quality guarantee is asserted in the indicative while the only procedure that could measure recall or false-positive rate is, verbatim, 'parallel, non-blocking ... not as a gate' and has never been run; ground-truth inspection confirms the sole worked example (examples/self-audit/) halts at cartography and contains no recall/FPR number — so by the document's own standard of proof the contribution is unestablished (rule 15 is itself violated: the document presents designed-but-never-run stages in the same present-indicative voice as the stages it actually ran, and does not label its own degraded validation status). (2) Soundness: the load-bearing inference 'isolation is the only thing that makes a same-model fleet more than N correlated draws' is a category error — isolation removes cross-talk but leaves N identically-distributed draws sharing every base-model blind spot — and the document supplies the defeating principle itself in rule 10 ('an LLM verifier shares the hallucination it is checking'), which it applies to force the quote-gate into code but then ignores for the six LLM-judged verification angles that the entire trust story leans on. (3) Integrity-overclaim: 'fabrication structurally impossible,' 'no fabricated results — ever,' 'impeccable,' and 'every argument, every word a checked invariant' are absolutes the mechanism cannot keep and that contradict rules 10, 14, and 15; the genuinely strong sub-claims (an UNTRACED number cannot enter; coverage of attention is logged; quotes are code-anchored) survive and should be KEPT, but stripped of the superlatives. The architecture is promising and several components are real and keepable; the validity of the headline contribution is not yet earned, and a code-verified fail-open in the one deterministic gate (F-007) plus a self-certifying flagship demonstration (F-079) mean the floor is the honest verdict regardless of delivery tone.

**Venue read.** `desk-reject-risk` — One completed, independently-graded result on third-party papers the tool did not author — a recall/false-positive pair plus a head-to-head against a human review panel — discharges the top objection and lets the comparative claims be stated (scoped to what was measured). With those, the bucket moves toward major-revision; without the number, every superlative remains assertion and the artifact stays desk-reject-risk on genre alone (it reads as a product/marketing spec, not a research contribution with question/method/data/result).

**Top strengths.**
- The deterministic, fail-closed quote-gate is a real, model-independent backbone: anchoring every non-absence finding to a code-checked verbatim substring is the right architecture and is genuinely stronger than an LLM self-check (rule 10 reasoning is sound where it is actually applied).
- Commit-and-reveal isolation (Delphi topology) genuinely removes inter-agent anchoring/herding — a real, keepable gain even though it does not, by itself, decorrelate shared-model error.
- The sentence-level coverage LEDGER (every disjoint range assigned to and returned by a reviewing agent) is a useful, auditable artifact — once it is renamed to what it proves (coverage of attention).
- The Execution-Provenance Wall's core — no UNTRACED number may enter the revised paper, with a deterministic provenance/consistency check — is a strong control that meaningfully raises the bar against invented-from-nothing numbers.
- Absence/silence findings as first-class, quote-exempt, never-demoted-for-being-unquotable is a correct and unusually honest design choice; the highest-severity problems often are what the paper never says.
- Rival-objective-function decorrelation (find-the-flaw vs find-the-strongest-version) is a sound mechanism in principle for suppressing sycophantic convergence — the design instinct is right; only the indicative-mood efficacy claim is premature.

**Kill shots (verbatim, desk-reject pre-mortem — routed around the chair).**

> This is how the paper dies: it sells the strongest efficacy claim in the field — a 300–600-agent run that is "better than any conference" and yields "the best feedback achievable" — while the document itself certifies, in writing, that the ONLY procedure that could produce a recall/false-positive number is "non-blocking" and "not a gate," i.e., the tool ships before a single measurement of whether it catches real flaws or manufactures false ones exists; the lone cited pilot is from a "build-small-first" verdict the same document says it "Supersedes" in favor of "go big," so the headline claims rest on a self-disclosed empty evidence base, and the first competent referee or replication attempt that asks "show me one run where it beat a human panel, and your false-positive rate" ends the paper on the spot. (F-073, desk-reject-premortem)

> The manuscript under review is the tool's own design document, the document was itself produced by the same Claude-subagent architecture it describes, and the self-audit is being run by that architecture on itself — while the ONLY out-of-model check (the 'external dissent leg') is, by the document's own rule, optional and 'skipped + disclosed if no compliant endpoint,' and the document states flatly there is 'no Bayesian mode; there is no exception' and the tool is 'Claude-only.' That is a closed epistemic loop: the same model class designs the tool, writes its claims, audits the claims, and verifies the audit, with no mandatory independent eye anywhere. A skeptical reviewer (or a journalist) will frame the eventual self-published 'we audited ourselves and passed' as exactly the conflict of interest peer review exists to prevent, and any later external failure becomes a story about a self-certifying system that marked its own homework — the most reputationally damaging way for an integrity-branded tool to fail. (F-079, desk-reject-premortem)

> The 'completeness certificate' proves only that some seat *returned a verdict for* every sentence range — it cannot and does not certify that every error was found, yet the document markets it as 'a printed guarantee every claim and sentence was examined' and the reason the tool 'beats any conference.' This is coverage theater: `covered == total` is satisfied by a seat glancing at a range and finding nothing, so a fatal flaw sitting inside a 'covered' sentence yields a 100%-coverage certificate that is simultaneously wrong. The catastrophic future writes itself: the tool issues a completeness certificate, the author submits trusting 'every word was examined,' a referee finds an elementary error in a sentence the certificate marked covered, and the headline guarantee becomes the object of ridicule — the certificate's existence makes the miss look like negligence rather than oversight. (F-077, desk-reject-premortem)

> The document stakes the whole 'monumental fleet' value proposition on the premise that commit-and-reveal plus 'opposed objective functions' converts correlated same-model draws into genuine independence — and it provides no evidence this overcomes shared-model blind spots, while elsewhere conceding the exact failure mode that defeats it: 'An LLM verifier shares the hallucination it is checking.' If that is true for the quote angle (the reason the quote-gate must be a script), it is equally true for the logic, severity, decision-relevance, steelman, and factual-literature angles, which are ALL run by the same model family. A blind spot common to the base model is invisible to 600 copies of it and to every LLM verifier downstream, no matter the role labels; scaling the fleet then buys correlated false confidence, not coverage. This is the failure mode that produces the catastrophic version of this tool: a confident 'monumental' green light on a paper with a flaw the model class systematically cannot see. (F-075, desk-reject-premortem)

> The document repeatedly asserts that the Runner/Scribe split makes fabrication 'structurally impossible' and 'Invented numbers are structurally impossible,' but the separation is enforced only by instructions to two prompts of the same model — there is no type system, OS boundary, or external referee that physically prevents a 'Scribe' context from emitting a number that no provenance token backs. 'Cannot' here means 'is told not to,' which is exactly the kind of claim a single counterexample falsifies. (F-074, desk-reject-premortem)

**Minority report.** Preserved intact (S10, opposed-incentive defender, F-054/F-055): the rival-objective decorrelation mechanism is genuinely sound in principle and survives steelmanning, contra the strongest form of the S09/desk-reject correlated-error critique. Conditioning a same-weights head on an opposed objective function ('find the fatal flaw' vs 'find the strongest defensible version') really does steer the search direction, so two such heads are NOT i.i.d. draws from one prior and the design is more than disciplinary 'costume' — S10 concedes the irreducible shared-substrate floor (a flaw class the base model cannot perceive is missed by all seats alike) but maintains that opposed objectives demonstrably reduce convergence-by-agreeableness and broaden the search, which is a real, keepable gain the majority verdict should not average away. The dissent's own caveat is what keeps it honest: the ONLY thing wrong with the claim is its indicative mood, not its substance. The central crux is therefore empirical and cheap to settle, and both S09 and S10 agree on the deciding diagnostic: run the planted-flaw harness and report the SHARED-MISS RATE — the fraction of planted flaws missed by ALL isolated seats — split by whether a 'find-the-flaw' seat and a 'find-the-strongest-version' seat independently fired. If opposed-objective pairs miss the same flaws at near the rate of identical-objective pairs, S09 wins and the trust claim must be scoped to the code gates; if added opposed-objective seats drive shared-miss toward zero on subtle plants, S10's 'real disagreement' survives as a MEASURED property. The document's existing inter-seat OVERLAP diagnostic cannot settle this — it measures overlap of detections, blind to shared misses (F-052) — so the instrument the manuscript offers as proof of decorrelation is the wrong one.

## Central tensions the panel surfaced

1. Decorrelation vs. correlated draws: the entire value of a 'fleet' over a single strong review rests on the claim (C-033) that commit-and-reveal plus opposed objective functions make a same-model fleet 'more than N correlated draws' — but every seat and every verifier is the same Claude model sharing the same priors and blind spots. Does the architecture genuinely decorrelate, or does it relaunder one model's judgment with extra steps?
2. Absolute guarantees vs. probabilistic reality: the design asserts hard invariants — 'Invented numbers are structurally impossible' (C-040, C-060), 'impeccable revised paper' (C-005), 'No fabricated citations, numbers, quotes, data, or results — ever' (C-018). Yet the Runner/Scribe wall, the quote-gate, and the panel are all enforced by prompts and one substring script. Are these enforced invariants or aspirational rules the model can still violate (e.g., a Runner producing a hash-stamped number from wrong/misread code)?
3. Exhaustiveness claims vs. what is actually checked: 'every argument, every word' (C-003), 'covered == total' (C-022, C-055), and 'better than any conference' (C-016) are presented as checked invariants. But a per-range verdict proves a range was visited, not that its argument was meaningfully reviewed; is the coverage certificate substantive assurance or coverage theater at scale?
4. Act II safety vs. error-laundering: re-running the author's code and auto-implementing presentation/additive edits, gated only by Claude verifiers plus author sign-off, is the differentiator (C-027). Does the Execution-Provenance Wall plus author ratification actually protect the scientific record, or can it lend a veneer of provenance to a mis-specified or non-reproducing pipeline?

### F-007 · High · claim-support · S02
- **Location:** helpers/quote_gate.py (helper, as concatenated) + prompts/shared_grounding_rules.md (the constitution) (s309)
- **Quote:** “An LLM verifier shares the
    hallucination it is checking, so this gate is a **script**, fail-closed.”
- **Issue:** The gate is claimed to be 'fail-closed', but its batch path fails OPEN for a malformed finding. In cmd_batch, the exempt branch only triggers when finding_type=='absence-silence' AND the quote normalizes to empty; otherwise control falls to check(quote, source), and check() itself returns (True, 'exempt-absence') for ANY quote that normalizes to empty (including a whitespace-only quote). I verified this directly against the actual helper: a finding typed 'statistical' with quote '   ' yields matched=True, match_level='exempt-absence', and cmd_batch assigns it severity_hint='keep' — i.e. an empty/whitespace quote on a NON-absence finding is reported as a passing, exempt match rather than failing closed. A fabricated or malformed finding that carries no real anchoring quote is therefore waved through with the same verdict as a legitimate absence-silence finding, defeating the one deterministic line of defense the design leans on. (Reachability caveat: this requires an upstream finding to reach the gate with an empty/whitespace quote and a non-absence type; the gate is nonetheless asserted to be fail-closed unconditionally, so the script not failing closed is a defect on the design's own terms.)
- **Why it matters:** C-013 and C-038 make the deterministic, fail-closed quote-gate the load-bearing reason a same-model fleet can be trusted ('not an LLM'; 'fail-closed'). If the script returns a passing 'exempt-absence' verdict for an empty quote on a non-absence finding, the guarantee that every non-absence finding is anchored by a verbatim quote does not actually hold in code.
- **Proposed fix:** In check(), only return the exempt-absence short-circuit when the CALLER declares the finding is absence-typed; otherwise an empty normalized quote must return (False, 'none'). Concretely, move the empty-quote exemption entirely into cmd_batch's is_absence branch and have check() treat nq=='' as a non-match (fail-closed). Add a unit test asserting that a non-absence finding with an empty/whitespace quote returns matched=False.

### F-008 · High · robustness · S02
- **Location:** helpers/verification_panel.md (the multi-angle gate) — Principles + Delivery rule (Act I) (s366, s385)
- **Quote:** “A finding that cannot survive a determined defense should not ship.”
- **Issue:** The steelman-charity angle is given veto power over true findings, and at the default tier nothing redundant or deterministic checks it. The table entry is 'steelman-charity ... strong defense ⇒ reject', and the Act-I delivery rule says a finding is delivered iff '... it is not killed by a strong steelman defense ...'. Whether a defense is 'strong' is an LLM judgment, not code. At 'thorough' the panel runs only '≥1 verifier on each soft angle', so a SINGLE charity verifier — the one angle explicitly instructed to argue FOR the paper — can unilaterally suppress a genuinely fatal finding, which is then routed to rejected_suggestions and never delivered to the author. The design correctly worries (C-038) that an LLM verifier shares the hallucination it checks, yet here an LLM is the sole gatekeeper deciding whether real criticism dies.
- **Why it matters:** This is squarely the failure mode my jurisdiction names: the steelman/charity angle CAN veto true findings. A suppressed High finding never reaches the user, so the tool's central promise (a 300-600-agent run will not launder a fatal flaw into a green light, C-012) is defeated not by missing the flaw but by a single charity-verifier killing it after it was found.
- **Proposed fix:** Make steelman-charity non-vetoing on its own: a 'strong defense' should DOWNGRADE-with-record or route the finding to the author with the defense attached, not silently reject it, mirroring how a failed quote-locator forces needs-author-confirmation rather than deletion. If charity must be able to reject, require ≥2 independent charity verifiers to concur even at 'thorough', and never let charity reject a finding the author has not seen. Persist every charity-rejected High/Med finding visibly (not only in rejected_suggestions) so the author can override.
- **Verification status:** `logic-checked`

### F-010 · High · absence-silence · S02
- **Location:** helpers/verification_panel.md (the multi-angle gate) — Principles ('Blind', 'One angle per verifier')
- **Issue:** The design uses the shared-hallucination argument to justify a CODE quote-gate ('An LLM verifier shares the hallucination it is checking, so this gate is a script') but never applies the same argument to the six LLM verification angles (logical-validity, factual-literature, severity-calibration, decision-relevance, steelman-charity, fix-safety), which are all the same base model. The only decorrelation offered for the panel is blindness ('a verifier that can see another's verdict is contaminated') and 'one angle per verifier'. Blindness removes the verdict-leakage correlation channel but does nothing about the dominant correlation source the document itself identifies elsewhere: same-model seats are 'N correlated draws' sharing a common prior. A shared blind spot — a method the base model misjudges, a citation it consistently misremembers, a fallacy it does not register — will be missed by every angle simultaneously, and no amount of angle-diversity or blindness catches an error all instances share. The manuscript is silent on why the very argument that forced the quote-gate into code does not also undercut the LLM panel, and offers no empirical decorrelation evidence (no inter-verifier disagreement rate, no planted-error catch rate per angle) for the panel as it does promise for the seats (the C-068 ablation targets recall/FPR of findings, not verifier independence).
- **Why it matters:** The panel (grounding rule 8; C-043, C-044) is the gate that lets the tool be 'monumental without being a confident-nonsense generator'. If its independence is only verdict-blindness on a single shared model, then for any error the base model reliably makes, the panel provides no protection — the same structural weakness the design explicitly rejects for quote-checking. This is the load-bearing soundness question for the whole verification mechanism and it is unaddressed.
- **Proposed fix:** Add an explicit treatment of same-model verifier correlation: (a) state plainly that the LLM angles share a common prior and therefore cannot catch errors the base model reliably makes, scoping the panel's guarantee accordingly; (b) route the highest-stakes angles to a deterministic check wherever possible (extend the code-backed set), and where not possible, use a different model or the consent-gated external dissent leg for at least one angle; (c) report an empirical inter-verifier decorrelation diagnostic (per-angle catch rate on planted errors, cross-verifier disagreement rate) alongside the seat-level recall/FPR validation, so the independence claim is measured, not asserted.
- **Verification status:** `needs-author-confirmation`
